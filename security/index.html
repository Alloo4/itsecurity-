What are healthcare cybersecurity and compliance services?

Healthcare cybersecurity and compliance services combine people, technology, and proven processes to protect patient data, keep clinical systems available, and show regulators and auditors that the right controls are in place. ITSN delivers them end to end, from assessment and architecture to continuous monitoring and incident response, for healthcare, health tech, and manufacturing organizations.

We are vendor-neutral. We assess where you’re exposed, close the gaps with best-fit technology, and build around the security tools you already own wherever they’re effective.

How do we improve security without disrupting clinical operations?

Security controls fail when they get in the way of care. We design controls that protect what matters without slowing the people who keep operations running, and we validate changes with the clinical and operational teams who know how systems are really used.

In practice that means phasing the work: starting with the highest-risk areas, running new rules in monitor-only mode before enforcing them, and scheduling changes in approved maintenance windows.

How do we prepare for a HIPAA security assessment or audit?

Preparation starts with knowing where your data lives, how it moves, and where the biggest risks are. A security assessment and gap analysis maps that picture and turns it into a prioritized list of fixes, with the documentation an auditor will ask for.

We design around the frameworks you answer to. Examples include HIPAA, HITECH, SOC 2, PCI DSS, CMMC, COBIT, NIST CSF, and NIST 800-53. Compliance is the baseline, not the finish line: for the wider picture, see our guide to cybersecurity services for healthcare.

How do we contain an incident instead of shutting everything down?

A network that was never built to be divided leaves one option during an attack: take everything offline. Zero-trust and network security work changes that. By mapping what talks to what, segmenting the network, and narrowing vendor access, you can isolate one area instead of the whole organization.

Our article on healthcare network security best practices walks through the steps, and our Voice & Data practice designs the networks those controls run on.

Should we build security in-house or use a managed partner?

Most organizations don’t have the budget or staffing to run a full security team around the clock. We can run a point-in-time assessment, provide continuous monitoring and threat detection, or do both. Our guide to healthcare managed security services explains what to expect from a managed partner.

When you need specific skills inside your own team, our staff augmentation service provides security and compliance specialists who work within your processes.
