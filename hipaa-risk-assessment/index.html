What is a HIPAA security risk assessment?

A HIPAA security risk assessment, also called a security risk analysis, is the accurate and thorough review of risks to electronic protected health information (ePHI) that the HIPAA Security Rule requires. It identifies where ePHI is created, stored and sent, what threatens it, how well current safeguards hold up, and which risks to fix first. ITSN delivers it as a scoped engagement for hospitals, clinics, health tech companies and the vendors that serve them.

Who needs a HIPAA security risk assessment?

Every covered entity, such as hospitals, physician groups, clinics and health plans, needs one. So does every business associate that creates, receives, maintains or transmits ePHI on their behalf, including health tech, billing, IT and cloud vendors. The requirement sits in the Security Rule's administrative safeguards at 45 CFR 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A).

Why does a HIPAA risk analysis matter now?

Risk analysis is where regulators look first. HHS's Office for Civil Rights launched its Risk Analysis Initiative in October 2024 to enforce this requirement. By October 2026 it had announced its 14th enforcement action under it, and failure to conduct a risk analysis remains one of the most frequently cited violations.

Proposed amendments to the Security Rule have moved to HHS's long-term agenda, with final action estimated for July 2027. Until then the current rule remains fully enforceable.

How often should we do a HIPAA security risk assessment?

The Security Rule does not set a fixed interval. It expects risk analysis to be ongoing and updated when your environment changes, such as a new EHR, a cloud migration, an acquisition, a new site or a security incident. Many organizations review it at least once a year so the risk register and remediation plan stay current.

Is the free HHS SRA Tool enough?

The HHS Security Risk Assessment Tool is a good starting point for small practices. It walks you through questions, but it does not inspect your systems, test network segmentation or tell you which fixes reduce the most risk for your budget. An outside assessment adds technical validation and an independent, documented view.

How is a risk assessment different from a SOC 2 audit or a penetration test?

A risk assessment covers every risk to ePHI across administrative, physical and technical safeguards, and it is what HIPAA requires. A penetration test attacks specific systems to prove whether a weakness can be exploited. A SOC 2 audit is an independent attestation that customers often ask vendors for. Many health tech companies need all three, and the risk assessment shows where to start. Read more in SOC 2 vs HIPAA compliance.
